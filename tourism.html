Tourism in the DPRK

The DPRK has traditionally welcomed international tourists through authorised travel companies, which arrange comprehensive guided programmes across the country. Tourist itineraries range from three days and two nights to fifteen days and fourteen nights, covering Pyongyang and provincial destinations.

The country's borders were closed to international tourism from January Juche 109 (2020) as an early and comprehensive measure against COVID-19. While the DPRK has gradually returned to international engagement in other areas, organised tourism for foreign nationals remains limited as of Juche 115 (2026). Visitors to the DPRK — when travel is available — do so through specialist tour operators working with the Korea International Travel Company.

Types of Tourism

Tourism in the DPRK is divided into ordinary tourism focused on sightseeing and special-interest tourism. Ordinary tourism includes visits to monumental edifices, museums and exhibition halls, as well as tours of scenic spots and historical remains.

Special-interest tourism has included archaeological tours centring on Koguryo's mural tomb clusters (a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2004), geological study tours, tours focusing on agriculture, traditional medicine, education and culture, and birding tours at Taedong River wetlands. Routes typically link Pyongyang with destinations such as Kaesong, Panmunjom, Myohyang Mountain, Mount Kumgang and Mount Paektu.

Pyongyang

The capital Pyongyang is the centrepiece of any DPRK visit. Key sites include the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun — the mausoleum of President Kim Il Sung and Chairman Kim Jong Il — the Tower of the Juche Idea on the Taedong River, and the Arch of Triumph, which at 60 metres surpasses its Parisian counterpart. Mangyongdae, birthplace of President Kim Il Sung, is one of the most visited sites.

The Pyongyang Metro, notable for its elaborately decorated stations at depths of up to 100 metres, is a highlight for many visitors. Recent years have seen major new developments in the city including Ryomyong Street (opened 2017), Mirae Scientists Street, the Sci-Tech Complex on Ssuk Island, and the Masikryong Ski Resort near Wonsan — reflecting the DPRK's investment in modern infrastructure.

Scenic Highlights beyond Pyongyang

Mount Paektu — the highest peak on the Korean peninsula at 2,744 metres and the country's most sacred mountain — is a destination of profound cultural, historical and revolutionary significance. Its volcanic crater lake, Lake Chon, is renowned for its beauty and is a major draw for visitors.

Mount Kumgang (Diamond Mountain), near the east coast, is celebrated for its dramatic granite peaks, waterfalls and valley scenery. The Myohyang Mountain area in the northwest houses the International Friendship Exhibition, which displays gifts presented to President Kim Il Sung and Chairman Kim Jong Il by foreign leaders and organisations from across the world. The coastal city of Wonsan, with its beach resort area, and the ancient capital Kaesong — home to Koryo-era heritage sites — round out the major tourist destinations.
