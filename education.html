Universal Free Education

Education at all levels is free in the DPRK. In September Juche 101 (2012), the Supreme People's Assembly promulgated the Law on Universal 12-Year Compulsory Education, which came into full effect in the following academic year. University students receive state-paid scholarships.

The 12-year compulsory system covers all children and youth from age 5 to 17 and consists of: one year of pre-school, five years of primary school, three years of junior middle school, and three years of senior middle school. Graduation from this system is a universal right regardless of region or background.

Structure of the Education System

A democratic educational system was established in December Juche 35 (1946). Universal compulsory primary education was introduced in Juche 45 (1956), universal secondary education in Juche 47 (1958), and universal 11-year compulsory education in Juche 61 (1972) — before the current 12-year system was adopted.

A well-regulated study-while-you-work system involving factory colleges, farm colleges and fishermen's colleges is operated across the country, alongside an expanding online education network. These provide lifelong learning pathways for all working people.

Higher Education and Science

The DPRK has numerous universities and institutions of higher learning. Kim Il Sung University, established in 1946, is the country's flagship comprehensive university. Other prominent institutions include Kim Chaek University of Technology, Pyongyang University of Foreign Studies, and a nationwide network of specialised institutes.

In recent years, the country has invested heavily in science and technology education. The Sci-Tech Complex, opened in Pyongyang in Juche 104 (2015), serves as a state-of-the-art centre for science popularisation and online learning — a digital library and study hub available to citizens of all ages.

Extracurricular and Cultural Education

Students have access to a rich array of extracurricular activities through schoolchildren's palaces and youth centres found in cities and counties throughout the country. The Mangyongdae Schoolchildren's Palace and the Pyongyang Schoolchildren's Palace are among the largest, offering tuition in music, fine arts, sports, robotics, and science.

The Grand People's Study House in central Pyongyang — a vast library and educational complex — along with multi-functional sci-tech diffusion bases spread across provinces, ensures that citizens have access to knowledge and continuing education throughout their lives. The country continues to increase investment in education and aims to build a "talented nation" well versed in modern science and technology.
