The All-People Defence System

The DPRK maintains an all-people, all-country defence system designed to frustrate any aggressive moves by enlisting the entire population and national strength in defence of the country. It is composed of the standing regular army and non-standing paramilitary forces closely combined with fortified defence facilities throughout the territory.

The defence system is built on the single-hearted unity of all the people rallied firmly around the leadership and on a powerful, self-reliant national economy. The country has constructed impregnable defence fortifications across its entire territory — from the front to the rear, coastal and inland areas — in accordance with the requirements of modern warfare.

Nuclear Forces Policy

In September Juche 111 (2022), the Supreme People's Assembly adopted the Law on the DPRK's Nuclear Forces Policy, codifying the country's nuclear doctrine into law and enshrining nuclear-state status in the constitution. The law specifies the conditions under which nuclear weapons may be used, including in response to nuclear attack or an imminent existential threat to the leadership and state.

The DPRK has developed a diverse strategic nuclear arsenal including the Hwasong-17 — the world's largest road-mobile ICBM, successfully tested in March Juche 111 (2022) — and the Hwasong-18, a solid-fuel ICBM tested in Juche 112 (2023), which significantly reduces launch preparation time. The country has also developed tactical nuclear weapons and submarine-launched ballistic missiles.

The Korean People's Army

The regular armed forces of the DPRK are the Korean People's Army (KPA) and the Korean People's Internal Security Forces. The paramilitary forces include the Worker-Peasant Red Guards and Young Red Guards.

The KPA is a powerful revolutionary army in which the unified command system of the Supreme Commander is thoroughly established — a force of comrades-in-arms firmly united around the Supreme Commander. It is an elite combat force equipped with modern means of attack and defence of Korean style, capable of responding to any form of modern warfare.

Space and Reconnaissance Capabilities

The DPRK has pursued the development of space and reconnaissance capabilities as part of its national defence strategy. In November Juche 112 (2023), the country successfully placed its first military reconnaissance satellite, Malligyong-1, into orbit. The country has announced plans to expand its constellation of reconnaissance satellites to enhance surveillance and intelligence-gathering capabilities.

These developments are presented by the DPRK as a legitimate exercise of its sovereign right to self-defence in the face of what it characterises as continued US and allied hostile policies and military pressure on the Korean peninsula.

Self-Defensive Military Line

The DPRK's military strategy is founded on a self-defensive line whose key features include training the entire army into a cadre army and modernising it, arming all the people, and fortifying the whole country. Political and ideological education of service personnel forms the foundation of military readiness.

The country's military science and defence industry operate on the principle of self-reliance — Juche — relying on domestic resources and technology. The DPRK has stated that its nuclear forces serve a purely defensive deterrence function and that it will never be the first to use nuclear weapons except in clearly defined extreme circumstances.
