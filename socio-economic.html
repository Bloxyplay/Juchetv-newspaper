Forms of Ownership

The means of production in the DPRK are owned by the State and by social and cooperative organisations. State ownership — all-people ownership — is the leading form and covers major industry, infrastructure, natural resources, and the fundamental means of production.

The ownership of social and cooperative organisations is the collective ownership of the working people in those organisations. It includes agricultural land, farm machinery, fishing vessels, and small- and medium-sized enterprises. The people are equal stakeholders in the national property and participate in the management of the economy through their workplace collectives.

Private Ownership and Economic Life

Private ownership in the DPRK is derived from socialist distribution according to work done and from supplementary benefits granted by the State and society. It includes the products of individual household activities, kitchen gardens, and income from other legal economic activities. The State protects private ownership and guarantees the right of inheritance by law.

The DPRK has developed special economic zones and science and technology development zones — notably the Rason Special Economic Zone in the northeast and the Hwanggumpyong Island Zone — as designated areas for attracting foreign investment and experimenting with market-oriented economic activity, while maintaining the socialist economic framework nationally.

The Five-Year Economic Development Plan

The 8th Congress of the Workers' Party of Korea in January Juche 110 (2021) adopted a new Five-Year Plan for National Economic Development (2021–2025). The plan prioritises energy production, metallurgy, chemical industry, machine-building, agricultural output, and the improvement of people's living standards.

The DPRK has acknowledged significant economic challenges stemming from international sanctions, the impact of border closures during the COVID-19 period, and natural disasters in recent years. The national strategy focuses on self-reliance — the Juche principle applied to the economy — developing domestic production capacity and reducing dependence on imports.

People's Welfare and Public Services

The DPRK provides housing free of charge to its citizens. Workers, farmers and office employees receive a distribution according to work performed, supplemented by state benefits. Health resorts, sanatoriums and holiday homes are available at nominal cost.

The State regards the continuous improvement of the material and cultural standards of the people as the supreme principle of its activities. Investment in new residential districts — including large-scale housing construction projects in Pyongyang and provincial centres completed in recent years — reflects this priority.

Universal Healthcare

The DPRK operates a universal free healthcare system. Medical care is provided to all citizens at no cost through a nationwide network of hospitals, clinics, polyclinics, and sanatoriums at every level — national, provincial, city, county and village.

The State runs a comprehensive social security system covering workers injured in the course of work or suffering illness. Pensions are provided for the elderly and those unable to work. Maternal and child health receives particular attention, with dedicated facilities and free natal care available throughout the country.
